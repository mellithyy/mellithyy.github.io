Sales Data Warehouse

Six raw files into one SQL Server star schema

The problem

Sales, customer and product data sat in two systems, a CRM and an ERP, as six raw files.

What I built

A SQL Server warehouse in three layers (bronze, silver, gold) with a star schema and checks after every layer.

The result

60,398 sales rows in one star schema; every check after every layer comes back clean.

What it is

A course project, January 2026: a small data warehouse in SQL Server. Two systems’ files are loaded as they are, cleaned, and joined into one star schema that analysts can query with plain SQL.

The question

Sales, customers and products arrive from two systems as six CSV files. The same customer carries three different ID formats, names have stray spaces, codes stand in for words, dates are stored as numbers, and some sales do not equal quantity times price.

Analysts need one clean model they can trust, and a way to show it is still clean after every load.

What it does

Six raw files

The CRM sends customers, products and sales lines; the ERP sends birthdates and gender, countries, and product categories. 116,294 rows in all, loaded into bronze exactly as they arrive.

One customer, three ID formats

The same person in the three files that describe customers, as they arrive, and as the gold layer holds him. The key has to be rewritten in two of the files before they can be joined.

FileKey as it arrivesWhat else it brings
  1. CRM cust_infoAW00011000“ Jon” and “Yang ” (stray spaces), marital status M, gender M
  2. ERP CUST_AZ12NASAW00011000birthdate 1971-10-06, gender Male
  3. ERP LOC_A101AW-00011000country Australia
  4. gold.dim_customerscustomer_key 1, AW00011000Jon Yang, Australia, Married, Male, born 1971-10-06

Cleans and standardises (silver)

Each rule is a line of SQL in one stored procedure. The chart counts the rows each one changed in these files, from the two empty product costs to the dashes taken out of all 18,484 location IDs.

Joins two systems into one model (gold)

Three customer files become one customer table, two product files one product table, and the sales lines the fact table. The CRM leaves 4,569 customers without a gender; the ERP fills 4,554 of them. Only current products go in: 102 older versions stay out.

Checks every layer

19 check queries after silver and gold. Most must come back empty; the rest list the values left in each coded column, to be read by eye. Rerun on the six files for this page, every check below still returns no rows.

Then asks it questions: sales over time

Thirteen analysis scripts run on the gold layer. By month, sales hold near half a million through 2011 and 2012, then jump in 2013, when the number of sales lines grows about fifteen times (3,397 in 2012, 52,782 in 2013). The first and last months are partial: the data runs from 29 December 2010 to 28 January 2014.

Part to whole

Which category brings the money: bikes are a quarter of the items sold but 96.5% of the sales. Accessories are six items in ten and 2.4% of the sales; clothing, 15% of the items and 1.2%.

Segments customers

Customers grouped as the analysis script defines them: VIP have 12 months or more of history and spent over 5,000; Regular have the history but spent less; New have under 12 months. Four in five customers are new.

How it works

  1. 01Two systems CRM: customers, products, sales lines. ERP: birthdates and gender, locations, categories
  2. 02Bronze Six tables, loaded as they are with BULK INSERT by one stored procedure that times each load
  3. 03Silver Six tables, cleaned and standardised by a second stored procedure
  4. 04Gold Three views: one fact table, sales, and two dimensions, customers and products
  5. 05Analysis Thirteen SQL scripts: change over time, running totals, performance, segments, part to whole
  6. 06Reports Two report views, one for customers and one for products, ready for a dashboard
Checked after each layer: 19 queries
  • After silver: keys, spaces, codes, dates, sums
  • After gold: unique keys, every sale linked

Every load builds each layer again from the one before it, so a fix in silver reaches gold on the next load.

The data model

The grain

One row per order line: an order number and a product, with sales, quantity and price. 60,398 lines.

Keys made in gold

Surrogate keys are numbered in the gold views, so the model does not depend on either system’s ID format.

One customer from three files

The CRM record wins; the ERP adds birthdate and country, and fills gender only where the CRM has none.

Model note

History is not kept: the course scope asks only for the latest data, so the product table holds current products only.

How I checked it

Each check is a query that should return nothing. If it returns rows, something upstream is wrong.

  • No empty or repeated keys in the cleaned customer and product tables.
  • No leading or trailing spaces left in text fields.
  • No negative or missing product costs, and no start date after its end date.
  • Order dates come before shipping and due dates.
  • Every sale equals quantity times price.
  • Every fact row finds its customer and its product.

What I would do differently

I would add row-count checks between the layers, so a load that drops rows fails loudly, and keep history for products and customers.
